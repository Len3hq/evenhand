# Assignment: greedy, least-loaded, track-matched, seeded

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-09-27 · Owner: B (built by the team's A under the whole-app plan)

## Context

T2 asks for "judge assignment". The fixtures arrive with 126 reviews and, on purpose, eight projects that only two judges finished. An organiser needs a way to give every project enough reviews, respect tracks and conflicts, spread the work fairly and be able to explain exactly why a judge got a project.

## Decision

- A pure function in the judging engine, `assign()` (BUILD-PLAN §9, decision 17). It repeatedly takes the project with the fewest reviews and gives it to the least-loaded judge who may review it, until every project reaches the target (3 by default) or runs out of eligible judges.
- A judge may review a project if they cover its track (a trackless project can go to anyone), have no conflict of interest with its team, and are not already assigned to it. The API adds every judge's own team to the conflicts, whatever the table says.
- **Seeded randomness.** Ties (which project, which judge) and each judge's queue order come from a seeded generator. The seed is returned, stored in the audit trail and can be passed back, so a run can be reproduced exactly. Random tie-breaks also make it harder to arrange who judges whom (Jecmen et al. 2020).
- **Top-up only.** Existing assignments are never moved or removed; a run adds what is missing, so it can be repeated after late submissions, new judges or track changes. Only submitted, eligible projects that are not a held or replaced duplicate copy are judged.
- **Honest report.** Projects that cannot reach the target are listed with how many judges could review them at all; the run also counts connected groups in the judge–project graph and warns when there is more than one, because normalisation cannot compare judges who share no projects.
- One transaction per run, audited: "ran assignment (3 reviews per project, seed …): 8 new assignments".

On the fixtures: 8 new assignments bring the eight two-review projects to 3; all 40 judged projects now have 3–5 reviews and every judge is in one connected group.

## Consequences (including what we gave up)

- Greedy, not optimal: it balances load well (tested: at most one apart when judges are interchangeable) but does not maximise overlap for calibration (stretch item 6).
- No per-judge capacity limit yet; the least-loaded rule keeps loads even instead.
- A judge whose tracks change keeps the assignments they already have.
